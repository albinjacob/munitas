Health organisation · Audit

Read every access decision, including every refusal

Every request to read data, from a person or a program, is recorded with who asked, what for, and why it was allowed or refused. A data protection officer reads that record and decides nothing. This walkthrough follows one officer from a home page of counts, to a list of every request, to the refusals alone with the reason for each.

  • Screenshots are real pictures of the Munitas console, the web app that people open in a browser. Click any picture to enlarge it.

Words used on this page

Organisation
A company or hospital group that uses Munitas. Each organisation sees only its own data.
Data protection officer
A person who watches how an organisation handles personal data. The role decides nothing about who may read it.
Access level
How restricted a dataset is. Raw is the most restricted, and nobody may read it without permission. Published is the most open.
Lease
Permission, given by the custodian, to read one dataset for one stated purpose and for a limited time.
Dataset
A named collection of records, such as a table of patient admissions.
Custodian
A person who holds the data custodian role, which allows deciding who may read data. A custodian decides for the departments they are an approver for.
Department
A team inside an organisation that owns some of its data, such as Cardiology.
Platform administrator
The person who runs Munitas itself. The role gives no say over who may read any department’s data.
Version
One sealed state of a dataset. Changing the contents means making a new version, never editing an old one.
Agent
A program registered on Munitas, with a named owner, that Munitas runs on somebody’s behalf instead of a person reading data directly.
Pipeline
A series of steps that Munitas runs on a dataset version, each step after the ones it depends on. The last step is a gate decision.
Filter
Keeping only the rows of a table that match values the custodian names, such as one patient’s id, so that other people’s rows do not leave with them.
Locked column
A column whose values are scrambled under a key made for each person. Reading them back needs permission. Destroying a person’s key later makes their values unreadable for good, while the sealed table itself never changes.
Privacy auditor
A person who checks how locked data was used. The role reads records of use and no personal data.

PART ONE

The record of every access decision

Every time somebody asks to read data, whether a person or a program, the platform decides and writes the decision down: who asked, what for, how restricted the data is, and the reason. The record keeps the refusals as well as the grants. A data protection officer is a person who watches how an organisation handles personal data. The role reads the record and decides nothing.

1
Nakamura
Nakamura's home page counts every decision and lists the latest refusals
Munitas console · Home · /

Nakamura is the data protection officer of the Health organisation. The home page of this role is about oversight and nothing else: it counts what has been asked, how much of it was refused, and shows the most recent refusals with the reason given for each. The access level of a dataset says how restricted it is, and Raw is the most restricted level. A lease is permission from the owning department to read one dataset for one stated purpose and for a limited time, and it ends by itself.

The heading is Welcome, Nakamura, and the line under it says You see every decision, including every refusal, and you make none. The first tile reads Requests recorded with Granted or not, every one is on record. The second reads Refused, with the share of everything asked beneath it. The other two tiles read Access currently granted and Records erased on request. Under Latest refusals a table lists who asked, what for, why it was refused and when, for example no role reaches class RAW, which means that no role may read data at the Raw level without a lease.
2
Nakamura
The Access decisions page lists every request, newest first
Munitas console · Access decisions · /audit

Nakamura follows the link to see every decision. Each request is recorded twice: once for whether the rules allowed it, and once for whether the access was actually given, and the two can differ. The columns say when, who asked and in what role, what was checked, the access level of the data, what it was for, the result and the reason. Only a data custodian, the data protection officer or a platform administrator may open this page: any other role is told that it is not for them, and the platform refuses it the data as well. The newest rows are not requests to read a dataset. They are Nakamura's own reads of the Locked data audit, and the privacy auditor Marlow's, because looking at the record is itself recorded, so the people who watch are watched in the same way.

The heading is Access decisions. The sentence under it begins Every request, and what happened to it. Two sets of buttons filter the list: All, Dataset access and Agent activity, and All, Refused and Allowed. The columns read When, Who, What was checked, Access level, What for, Result and Why. The first rows name Nakamura and Data protection officer, Whether it was allowed, Read the locked data audit: keys and the result yes. A count and page numbers at the foot say how many decisions there are.
3
Nakamura
One click shows only the refusals, with the reason for each
Munitas console · Access decisions · /audit, with Refused chosen

Nakamura chooses Refused, and every row that remains is a request that was turned down. Each one keeps its date and time, the person who asked and the role they held, the access level they reached for, what they said it was for, and the reasons in plain sentences. A reason can be that no role reaches the Raw level, or that a lease which once covered the request has since been revoked, which means that it was ended early. A program is held to the same record: the oldest row on this page is a pipeline action that was refused, with the reason that it did not present the credential of the task it was acting for. A link on each row opens the dataset version that was asked for.

The button Refused is chosen, and every row in the list has the result no in red. The reasons include “No role reaches class RAW.”, and a link, Open the dataset version, sits under each. The first row names Devi, Data engineer, the access level Raw, read locked columns and the reason This person is not an approver of the department that owns this data and holds no current lease for its locked columns. Rows for Sam name a revoked lease by its identifier.
