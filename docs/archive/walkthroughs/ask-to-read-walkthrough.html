How to read this page

  • Every step is one screen, or one moment, in a real run. The label under the title names where it happens, and the step reads in the same order each time: what is happening, the evidence, and then what to look for in it.
  • Screenshots are real pictures of the Munitas console, the web app that people open in a browser. Click any picture to enlarge it.
  • Every name of a person, patient or card holder in the example data is invented, and every screenshot is unedited apart from being cropped to the part that matters.

Words used on this page

Organisation
A company or hospital group that uses Munitas. Each organisation sees only its own data.
Department
A team inside an organisation that owns some of its data, such as Cardiology.
Dataset
A named collection of records, such as a table of patient admissions.
Access level
How restricted a dataset is. Raw is the most restricted, and nobody may read it without permission. Published is the most open.
Custodian
A person who holds the data custodian role, which allows deciding who may read data. A custodian decides for the departments they are an approver for.
Request
A written ask to read one dataset for one stated purpose. The custodian of the department that owns the dataset decides it.
Purpose
The reason a person states for reading data. A lease covers that purpose only, unless the custodian chooses otherwise.
Lease
Permission, given by the custodian, to read one dataset for one stated purpose and for a limited time.
Sealed
Finished and closed for good, so that it can never be edited.
Release
A decision to let a dataset version be read more widely than before. A release changes who may read it and moves no data.

PART ONE

A researcher asks, and a custodian decides

Sam is a researcher who wants to read an original recording that a data engineer has sealed. Sam's role does not read data at the Raw level, so the recording's page asks what the data would be used for before it shows anything more.

1
Sam
Sam opens the recording and finds a request form
Munitas console · Version detail · /versions/<id>

A request is a written ask to read one dataset for one stated purpose. The page offers a request form where a file would be, and says who decides.

Your access reads This is more sensitive than your role reads, so Hartley decides. The form asks for a purpose, a reason and a length of time.
2
Sam
Sam writes the purpose and the reason
Munitas console · Version detail · the request form

Sam states the purpose and why a less sensitive substitute would not do. Both are shown to whoever decides, and neither is kept private.

The purpose reads measure recall of the de-identification model before wider release, and the reason reads for arrhythmia detection study, comparing detected spans against the original audio.
3
Sam
The request goes to the custodian
Munitas console · Version detail · /versions/<id>

Sam cannot approve the request, and nothing is readable yet. The page says who it is waiting on.

Your access reads Your request has gone to the person answerable for this data. Nothing is readable until they grant it.
4
Hartley
Hartley finds Sam's request waiting
Munitas console · Home · the custodian's home page

Hartley's home page lists the requests to read Cardiology's data. A request for a recording that Radiology owns would never appear here. The decisions listed below the request were made earlier.

Waiting for your decision counts 1. The card reads Sam (Researcher) wants to read the recording, with Raw, Sam's reason, and Granting gives Sam 24 hours, for this purpose only.
5
Hartley
Hartley grants the request
Munitas console · Home · after granting

Granting creates a lease, which is permission to read one dataset for one purpose and for a limited time. The request leaves the waiting list and moves into the history of decisions.

A notice at the bottom right reads Access granted to Sam (Researcher), and Waiting for your decision counts 0.
